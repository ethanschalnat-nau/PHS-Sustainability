Project Description

The Ponderosa High School (PHS) sustainability project is to analyze and improve the current off-grid renewable system for the PHS greenhouse. PHS is a small high school located in Flagstaff, dedicated to bringing new opportunities and STEAM learning to their students. The renewable energy system currently has a wind turbine, four solar panels, and a 24-volt battery storage system. The PHS greenhouse is powered by their renewable energy system, keeping the plants and animals (a bunny, a tortoise, and fish) in a healthy environment. To improve the greenhouse, the team is increasing solar energy and adding human powered energy generation. The improvements will be used to have additional features (heat lamps, fans, and lights) in the greenhouse and continue STEAM learning for students at PHS.

Abstract

The Ponderosa High School (PHS) greenhouse is a hands-on STEAM-focused environment supporting plants, aquaponics, and animals using an off-grid hybrid renewable energy system. However, the existing solar, wind, and 24-volt battery system does not consistently meet load demands during the winter months. If all load demands are not fulfilled, biological life in the greenhouse is vulnerable to unstable living conditions. The PHS Sustainability project goal is to address the load fulfillment concerns by analyzing the existing system and improving the greenhouse as an integrated energy and educational system. For analysis, the team conducted an energy audit to define electrical loads, renewable generation, battery capacity, and system limitations. The results of the energy audit are used to design upgrades for the greenhouse infrastructure such as additional photovoltaic energy, increased battery capacity, integration of human-powered bicycle energy generation, and new electrical infrastructure. Simultaneously, the team develops STEAM-focused educational lessons that use the greenhouse and its renewable technologies to provide hands-on STEAM learning for the students at PHS. The integration of the human-powered bicycle energy generation combines the core concepts of the project by providing an interactive STEAM demonstration while also supplementing energy to the greenhouse. The improved system is intended to provide reliable monthly energy generation for greenhouse operations while supporting future expansion and suitable living conditions for biological life. By combining renewable-energy analysis, improvements to the off-grid hybrid system, and STEAM-focused renewable energy education, the project transforms the PHS greenhouse into a better sustainable greenhouse with interactive STEAM renewable energy education for the future generations of students at PHS.
